THE ARGUMENT OF ALPÖGE’S FORMAL PROOF OF THE RIEMANN HYPOTHESIS: AN ACCOUNT READ FROM THE LEAN SOURCE QUI VINCIT AND FABLE Abstract. On 20 November 2026 Levent Alpöge released a Lean 4 formalisation, 17 million lines long, of a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and of its analogue for every Dirichlet L-function, with a note saying that no human-readable proof has been written. We built the archive, twice. It compiles, the five top theorems depend on no axiom beyond the three on which Mathlib rests, and the kernel accepts every theorem, and every definition that is not marked unsafe or partial, of the import closure, 2.7 million declarations in all, replayed from an empty environment by Lean’s own checker and by a program of ours. This paper says what the argument is, as read from the Lean source. The exponent 12 comes from one optimisation: an upper bound, of the shape x/m + m, for a weighted count of prime powers whose Frobenius lies outside a CM type, where m is the length of a chain of theta lifts between definite unitary groups. The bound is a squeeze between two slopes of Hermitian lattices. Divisibility of periods at inert primes, which comes from the height-two formal group of a CM elliptic curve, raises the slopes of lattices of algebraic modular forms. A slope inequality for nearly tight frames of maps, which we state in full, transfers that to a lattice of periods. The slope of that lattice is bounded above by a period computation that returns exactly the main term of the count, through a dimension count and the Chowla–Selberg formula.
