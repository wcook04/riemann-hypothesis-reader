The argument of Alpöge’s formal proof of the Riemann Hypothesis: an account read from the Lean source

Abstract

On 20 November 2026 Levent Alpöge released a Lean 4 formalisation, 17 million lines long, of a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and of its analogue for every Dirichlet LLL-function, with a note saying that no human-readable proof has been written. We built the archive, twice. It compiles, the five top theorems depend on no axiom beyond the three on which Mathlib rests, and the kernel accepts every theorem, and every definition that is not marked unsafe or partial, of the import closure, 2.7 million declarations in all, replayed from an empty environment by Lean’s own checker and by a program of ours. This paper says what the argument is, as read from the Lean source. The exponent 12\tfrac1221​ comes from one optimisation: an upper bound, of the shape x/m+mx/m+mx/m+m, for a weighted count of prime powers whose Frobenius lies outside a CM type, where mmm is the length of a chain of theta lifts between definite unitary groups. The bound is a squeeze between two slopes of Hermitian lattices. Divisibility of periods at inert primes, which comes from the height-two formal group of a CM elliptic curve, raises the slopes of lattices of algebraic modular forms. A slope inequality for nearly tight frames of maps, which we state in full, transfers that to a lattice of periods. The slope of that lattice is bounded above by a period computation that returns exactly the main term of the count, through a dimension count and the Chowla–Selberg formula. We describe each step and each input, determine by machine which parts of the archive the final theorem uses, and identify the published theorems on which the proof rests.

1. Introduction

On 20 November 2026 Levent Alpöge released a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis (Riemann 1859), and of the generalised Riemann Hypothesis for the LLL-functions of Dirichlet (Lejeune-Dirichlet 1837), formalised in Lean 4 (Moura and Ullrich 2021) on top of Mathlib (The mathlib Community 2020). The release consists of the Lean source (Alpöge 2026a), 175,187 Lean files and 17,374,215 lines, and a four-page note (Alpöge 2026b) with a timeline of twenty steps. The note says that no human-readable proof has been written.

This paper does two things. It reports that the proof is correct as a formal object: we built the archive, it compiles, its five top theorems depend on the three standard axioms and nothing else, and the kernel accepts every theorem, and every definition that is not marked unsafe or partial, of the import closure, replayed from an empty environment by Lean’s own checker and by a program of ours (2). And it says what the argument is: an account read from the Lean source, from the final theorem down to the theorems of the published literature on which it rests. The release does not contain one.

The argument

Fix a primitive Dirichlet character χ\chiχ. The proof has four movements.

Reduction to a one-sided bound on windows. A windowed form of Landau’s theorem reduces the non-vanishing of L(s,χ)L(s,\chi)L(s,χ) for Re⁡s>12\operatorname{Re}s>\tfrac12Res>21​ to a bound G(x)≤x1/2+εG(x)\le x^{1/2+\varepsilon}G(x)≤x1/2+ε for a smoothed sum over prime powers, required only for xxx in long windows (4.1).

An exact identity. For an auxiliary abelian CM field with CM type Φ\PhiΦ, a weighted count ASK\mathrm{ASK}ASK of the prime powers whose Frobenius lies outside Φ\PhiΦ equals a main term MAIN\mathrm{MAIN}MAIN plus a combination of such sums (4.2). After averaging, an upper bound ASK ≤ MAIN+Err\mathrm{ASK}\ \le\ \mathrm{MAIN}+\mathrm{Err}ASK ≤ MAIN+Err becomes the bound on GGG, and the error has three parts of sizes x/mx/mx/m, mmm and a rank term, where mmm is a parameter. At m≈xm\approx\sqrt xm≈x​ they balance. This is the only place where 12\tfrac1221​ is produced (4.4).

A squeeze between two slopes. The upper bound is proved with Hermitian lattices. For each of many indices uuu there is a lattice MuM_uMu​ of algebraic modular forms on a definite unitary group, and a map BuB_uBu​ sending a form to the periods of its mmm-fold iterated theta lift; mmm is the number of lifts. At a prime ppp inert in the CM field those periods are divisible by about pa/p+a/p3+⋯p^{a/p+a/p^3+\cdots}pa/p+a/p3+⋯, where aaa is the order of the Taylor coefficients of theta functions through which they are expressed, because a CM elliptic curve has a formal group of height two there (6). So BuB_uBu​ still maps into the lattice PPP of periods a lattice NuN_uNu​, obtained by rescaling a sublattice of MuM_uMu​, whose slope exceeds that of MuM_uMu​ by at least the count ASK\mathrm{ASK}ASK, up to errors and to correction terms that cancel later. The maps BuB_uBu​ form a nearly tight frame, by a relative trace formula for Bessel–Poincaré series (7), and a slope inequality for such frames bounds the slope of PPP from below by the mean slope of the NuN_uNu​ (5.4). From above, the slope of PPP is bounded by mmm times log⁡(a!/Ωa)\log(a!/\Omega^a)log(a!/Ωa), with Ω\OmegaΩ a CM period, less a term from an auxiliary level prime; a dimension count for that prime and the Chowla–Selberg formula for the mean of log⁡Ω\log\OmegalogΩ turn this into MAIN\mathrm{MAIN}MAIN and a remainder that the correction terms cancel (5.5).

Three inputs. The construction needs a bound on Galois cohomology in a cyclotomic tower, which the note calls a weakening of Vandiver’s conjecture (8); the existence of suitable auxiliary fields at every scale in the windows (9); and a body of theorems on unitary groups, Shimura varieties and Arakelov geometry, each of which the archive proves for itself (10).

The shape x/m+mx/m+mx/m+m is that of Stepanov’s method (Stepanov 1969; Bombieri 1974; Schmidt 1976), where a polynomial is made to vanish to order mmm at the points to be counted. Here the multiplicity is the length of a chain of theta lifts, and the degree bound is replaced by slopes of lattices in the sense of Bost (Bost 1996, 2001; Chambert-Loir 2002).
